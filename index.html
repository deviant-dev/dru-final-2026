Marquis de Sade

Historical Figure  ·  Philosopher & Author
  • French aristocrat, philosopher, and author whose libertine life shaped kink history
  • Works like 120 Days of Sodom and Justine explored power, pain, and transgression through fiction
  • The term "sadism" was coined directly from his name by 19th-century sexologist Krafft-Ebing
  • Blended Enlightenment philosophy with extreme erotica — challenging religion, morality, and authority
  • Represents the literary and philosophical roots of modern BDSM
Origin of "sadism" Enlightenment transgression Foundational BDSM literature

Leopold von Sacher-Masoch

Historical Figure  ·  Author & Nobleman
  • Austrian nobleman whose 1870 novella Venus in Furs defined submissive fantasy in literature
  • Story follows a man who signs a real submission contract with a dominant woman — drawn from his own life
  • The term "masochism" coined from his name by Krafft-Ebing in Psychopathia Sexualis (1886)
  • Together with de Sade, frames the twin pillars of sadomasochism
  • Legitimized submission as a conscious, romantic, and deeply personal pursuit

Kinbaku / Shibari

Tradition  ·  Japan
  • Japanese rope bondage rooted in hojōjutsu, a martial restraint technique from the Edo period
  • Early 20th century artist Seiu Ito transformed rope tying into erotic and artistic expression
  • Emphasizes aesthetics, trust, and psychological intimacy between rigger and model
  • Spread globally in the 1990s–2000s under the name "shibari"
  • Now one of the most practiced and respected kink traditions worldwide
Roots in Edo-period Japan Seiu Ito — key pioneer Art + intimacy + trust

Tom of Finland

Artist  ·  LGBTQ+ Leather History
  • Finnish illustrator (Touko Laaksonen) whose bold erotic art defined the visual language of gay leather culture
  • Began drawing during WWII; published in physique magazines from the 1950s onward
  • Celebrated muscular men in leather, uniforms, and boots — defying the era's shame around homosexuality
  • Reached thousands of isolated gay men worldwide, offering validation and fantasy during criminalization
  • His foundation preserves his legacy; imagery appears in fashion, pride, and kink culture globally
Defined leather aesthetic Gay male kink visibility Art as resistance

TES — The Eulenspiegel Society

Organization  ·  Community Building
  • Oldest BDSM organization in the United States, founded in NYC in 1971 by Pat Bond and Fran Nowve
  • Named after Till Eulenspiegel — a prankster figure symbolizing those who "march to a different drummer"
  • Provided safe, confidential spaces for education and community when BDSM was criminalized and stigmatized
  • Published the influential newsletter Prometheus; hosted workshops, parties, and classes
  • Still active today — inspiring groups like the Society of Janus; championed kink as valid human expression

San Francisco, 1978 – 1983

Samois

Organization  ·  Lesbian Feminist BDSM
  • First known public lesbian BDSM organization in the United States, San Francisco 1978
  • Co-founded by Pat Califia and Gayle Rubin — named after the dominatrix's estate in Story of O
  • Challenged anti-SM strands of radical feminism — argued BDSM could be feminist and empowering
  • Produced What Color Is Your Handkerchief? and organized educational workshops and parties
  • Disbanded in 1983 but left a lasting legacy, inspiring all queer leather and leatherdyke communities
First US lesbian BDSM org Pat Califia & Gayle Rubin Feminist sex wars

Pony Play

Practice  ·  Pet Play & Animal Roleplay
  • One partner embodies a pony (harnesses, bits, tails, hooves); the other acts as trainer, rider, or owner
  • Blends power exchange, physical exertion, obedience, and performance — often including dressage and carriage pulling
  • Roots in ancient mythology; modern consensual practice emerged in 20th-century BDSM and leather scenes
  • Unique for its intersection of athletic performance and intimate D/s dynamic
  • Thrives today in dedicated communities with workshops on safe bit use, showmanship, and aftercare
